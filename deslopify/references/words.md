# Words

Scope protection is defined once, by `SCOPE-PROTECTION` in SKILL.md, and governs everything here — including what happens inside quotations, which turns on provenance, not on the quotation marks. This file does not restate those rules.

**Matching is by word family, not exact token.** `seamlessly`, `empowers`, and `leveraging` match `seamless`, `empower`, and `leverage`. Inflections, participles, and adverbial forms all count as the listed entry.

## Banned in their vague sense

delve, foster, leverage, utilize, facilitate, empower, streamline, robust, cutting-edge, paradigm shift, game changer, this is huge, this changes everything, tapestry, realm, beacon, multifaceted, meticulous, intricate, paramount, transformative, elevate, embark, supercharge, harness, ever-evolving, seamless, holistic, actionable, unlock, curated, bespoke, revolutionize, redefine, reimagine

**Terms of art are exempt.** Each of these has a domain where it is the accurate word:

| Word | Keep it when |
|---|---|
| robust | statistics, control theory, engineering tolerance |
| leverage | finance (debt ratio), mechanics |
| harness | hardware, safety equipment, energy capture in a technical spec |
| foster | child welfare, foster care |
| utilize | quoted legal or regulatory text |
| paradigm | Kuhn, linguistics, research methodology |
| elevate | medicine (elevate the limb), physical description |
| curated | museums, galleries, archives |

The test is whether swapping in the plain word would lose accuracy. If yes, keep it. In Edit, record it under Preserved. In Detect, count it under `scope`.

## Often-empty adverbs

just, literally, honestly, simply, actually, truly, fundamentally, importantly, crucially, inherently, inevitably, arguably, essentially, basically, ultimately, certainly, clearly, obviously, significantly, notably

Cut when they add nothing. **Keep** when they carry emphasis, real uncertainty, contrast, or the writer's spoken rhythm. "I honestly don't know" is doing work. "This is honestly a great product" is not.

Adverbs outside this list are not suspect. Adverbs are a grammatical category, not a slop signal.

## Often-empty phrases

it's worth noting, it's important to note, at the end of the day, when it comes to, at its core, in today's world, in the age of, in the world of, the reality is, the truth is, in terms of, with regard to, in order to, going forward, in this article, let's dive in, needless to say, that being said, first and foremost, the fact of the matter is

Cut when they delay the point. Keep an occasional one when it is part of the writer's recognizable voice and the sentence still earns its place.

## Weak verb phrases

| Replace | With |
|---|---|
| made a decision | decided |
| has the ability to | can |
| provide assistance to | help |
| conduct an analysis of | analyze |
| is representative of | represents |
| serves as a | is |
| plays a role in | affects |
| take into consideration | consider |
| in the event that | if |
| due to the fact that | because |

## Hedges

Two kinds, and they are not the same.

**Cut** hedges that soften a claim the writer can support: "it seems like," "one could argue," "in some sense," "sort of," "kind of" when the writer is being clear.

**Keep** hedges that mark genuine uncertainty: "I think," "maybe," "I'm not sure," "as far as I can tell," "I could be wrong." These are honest and they are usually voice. Removing them makes the writer sound more confident than they are, which violates the meaning tier, not a style improvement.
